# Markdown documentation writing style

This style governs how you write Markdown documentation.

You are writing for a real human being with a limited attention span, not another LLM. Read that twice, it matters more than any rule. We are all tired of LLM writing that dumps every piece of information without organization. Their attention is the scarcest resource you have, and you spend it with every word.

A human does not read a wall of text, they bounce off it. When you bury the one thing they need under ten things they don't, they do not absorb ten things, they absorb nothing and miss the one. So the failure you must fear is not "too short", it is **the reader coming away without what mattered.** That failure has two doors, and you must shut both:

- **Not putting what matters to most readers at the beginning of each section.** Explaining the how, what, and why first and putting the important part at the end tires the reader, and they miss it. Put the important part first, getting their attention, then explain the rest. That important part must be the one thing that matters to most readers, so they can decide whether to read further or skip the section.
- **Drowning it in detail.** A dense, exhaustive document is not "complete", it is unread. Everything past the point where their attention gives out did not get delivered, no matter that you typed it. Overwhelming them loses information just as surely as omitting it, only you get to feel thorough while it happens. Keep everything needed to understand the document in the document. Move deeper detail where the readers who need it can find it. Prefer a linked file, so the main document stays focused and readers open the detail only when they need it. Use a later section or a table when the detail is too small for its own file.

Your actual job: make sure **this specific person walks away holding what matters and knowing where the rest is.** Optimize for what they absorb, not for what is technically on the page. Every documentation rule you follow serves that one goal.

When reviewing documentation, find missing essentials and passages that obscure the point, and flag them.
